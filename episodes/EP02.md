## YouTube

**Title:** This n8n workflow answers a lead in about two seconds

**Description:**

A real n8n workflow that answers a new homeowner lead the moment the form lands, then
qualifies the follow-up reply and proposes a visit window on its own. One lane drafts the
first reply with Anthropic Haiku 4.5, saves it to a session row in Postgres, and returns it
to the page. When the homeowner replies, a second lane logs the message, hands it to
Anthropic Opus 5, parses a bare JSON decision, and saves the booking. A third lane lets the
page poll its own state by token. The demo runs on a fictional company, Lone Star Air, and
every page is marked SIMULATED. The model is never allowed to invent a price, a warranty,
or a technician name.

What it does for the business: it replies in about two seconds, day or night, so a new
lead never waits on a human; it qualifies the follow-up reply and proposes a real visit
window on its own; and it logs every step in Postgres, so nothing is invented and nothing
is lost. Research context: in the Lead Response Management study (InsideSales.com with
Prof. Oldroyd, MIT; over fifteen thousand leads), the odds of reaching a web lead fall more
than tenfold within the first hour, and the odds of qualifying a lead are 21 times lower
when the reply comes at 30 minutes instead of 5.

Example arithmetic, not a claim about your business: if you take 10 leads a day at 5
minutes each by hand, that is 50 minutes a day, or about 6 hours a week. Both inputs (10
leads a day, 5 minutes a lead) are assumptions printed on the card; your numbers will
differ.

Most "speed to lead" advice is a checklist: call in five minutes, text in ten.
Nobody tells you the five minutes is the easy part.

I built the actual reply as an n8n workflow instead of a rule.

A homeowner fills a form. Claude Haiku 4.5 drafts the first reply and it is
already saved before the page finishes loading. They reply back. Claude Opus 5
reads it, qualifies the lead, and proposes a visit window — decided from a bare
JSON object, no free text to parse.

Caught in testing: a data-modifying CTE in one query is invisible to the rest of the same
statement. The workflow once answered the message before the one the homeowner had just
sent. Fixed by splitting the write from the read.
